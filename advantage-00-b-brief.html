Advantage prototypes

Executive summary

What this is. A procurement portal for small and mid-sized Nordic firms. It reads their supplier invoices, shows what they already save through negotiated agreements, and points at money they are still leaving on the table.

Where we are. Nine generations of prototypes, from first sprint sketches to five builds that work end to end. Two questions are still open: which navigation model ships, and how plain the language needs to be.

What I would do next. Test the Worklist against the Scorecard with a real procurement manager. Those two make opposite bets on what he wants first, and no amount of internal review will settle it.

The Worklist

Works end to end

advantage-v9-worklist.html

The month's work as a numbered list of things to do.

Treats procurement as a job with tasks rather than a dashboard to interpret. The front page says “3 things to do. About 10 minutes.” Each row gives a plain heading, what it is worth in kroner per year, one paragraph of why, and two labelled buttons. Finishing one moves it to a Done list with a running total, so the pile visibly shrinks.

Written for a 61-year-old procurement manager: no software jargon, 18px body text, every figure written out as “118 000 kr a year”, every icon paired with a word. Four sections instead of seven.

Strongest bet if he wants to be handed his work. Weakest if he wants to understand his position before acting.

02

The Scorecard

Works end to end

advantage-v9-scorecard.html

Answers “am I doing well or badly” before anything else.

Opens with three labelled bars: You, Firms like yours, The best firms in your trade. Each figure is written twice, in kroner and in plain words (“you buy 62 of every 100 kroner at an agreed price”). Underneath: what the best firms do that you do not, as three things you can act on.

Because the comparison supplies the motivation, the word “potential” never appears. The bars are told apart by label and fill pattern, not colour, so they survive a black-and-white printout.

Strongest bet if he is a professional who wants his score first. For the new customer with no data, the comparison is the entire story, which makes this the best cold-start of the three.

The Letter

Works end to end

advantage-v9-letter.html

A letter from his advisor, in full sentences.

No charts, no tiles, no status markers. Mette writes to him: what she read, what she found, and one question at a time with a clear yes and a clear not-now. Answering reveals the next paragraph. Signed with a name and a phone number.

The most radical departure and the cheapest to test. Worth showing even if it loses, because his reaction tells you how much explaining the product actually needs.

04

The Ledger

Works end to end

advantage-v8-ledger.html

Three panes, keyboard driven, everything comparable at a glance.

Sidebar, then a list of all 14 categories with the data inside the rows as coverage bars, then a detail pane. No chart on the overview at all. Arrow keys move the selection with the detail pane keeping pace and no page reload, Enter opens the agreement, ⌘K filters live.

The complete feature set: agreement pages with how-to-buy, category pages, notifications with editable rules, the advisor inbox, the activity feed. The better tool of the two v8 builds.

Its main advantage is invisible if nobody touches the keyboard. Worth watching for in testing.

05

The Bento

Works end to end

advantage-v8-bento.html

Seven tiles where size is the only ranking device.

A 3-column grid of unequal tiles, each a link into its section, with drill-down animating from the tile's own position. Exactly one data mark per tile and each mark type used once. A reorder mode with keyboard controls.

Same complete feature set as the Ledger. The better first impression of the two; the weaker tool. Missing global search and keyboard navigation, which exist only in the Ledger.

06

Today

Worth opening

advantage-v7-4-today.html

A queue of prepared decisions, and the only build where rules create work.

Six decisions, each stating exactly what it commits you to, with three levels: approve, request an offer, or ask the advisor. Advisor time is metered against the plan.

Worth opening for one thing: change the expiry rule from 90 to 120 days and a real new decision card appears. That causal link between a setting and tomorrow's work is the most convincing single mechanic anyone built here, and it did not survive into the v8 builds. It should.

Five layout options

Design reference

advantage-dashboard-layouts.html

Wireframes and reasoning for five ways to structure the overview.

Vault, Bento, Ledger, Focus Card and Atlas, each with its structural breakdown and an argument for how it moves the user from the total savings figure to one agreement. Not interactive.

Bento and Ledger were built. Three remain unbuilt: the scroll narrative, the single-decision card, and the spend treemap. The Atlas in particular is the only one that makes the gap visible as a shape before you read anything.

Gap analysis

Read first

advantage-GAP-ANALYSIS.md

What the competitors ship, and the sixteen gaps ranked.

Vinst and Seiva both sell breadth, a named human and proof. Neither shows any evidence of notifications, messaging, document storage, roles and permissions, ordering workflows, or reporting. Four of those are now built here; the rest are open.

Top of the remaining list: multi-user roles and approvals, because every build still assumes one person is the company.
