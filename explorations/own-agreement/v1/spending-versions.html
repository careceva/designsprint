The spending page, as a journey

The hero from the first round stays. The body is now two acts: what you spend and save today, in the areas where an agreement is landing, then what you spend elsewhere and what the negotiated rate would save there. Type a figure in act two and the saving beside it, and the act's total, follow at once.

  • Act one is verified. What ran through each agreement, read off the invoice, and what it saved, in green. Its total is the hero's "Through an agreement" figure. No share of an area is shown, because for a documents-only account we do not know the area.
  • Act two is a projection. A figure we read prints exact, in blue; the industry median prints as a band; a figure you type prints as a band too, because a typed spend is a tune, never a verified figure.
  • Not reviewed turns into Save the moment a row's figure changes; Saved, With Peter and Not relevant follow from the row's actions. Not relevant drops the area from the total, with Undo.

Version 1

Open the row

Two tables. Today: area, what ran through the agreement, saved. Elsewhere: area, spend, what you could save, status. A row in the second table opens to the form from the Overview; the spend field is in the form, and every keystroke moves the saving on the row above it and the total in the heading.

What to weigh
  • The number you are correcting sits on the row, and you type its replacement one level down. The eye travels.
  • The closed table is calm: no inputs until you ask for one.
  • Save and Send to review live in the open row, where the supplier is.
Version 2

Type in the row

The same two tables, but in the second one the spend cell is the input. The industry band is its placeholder, a figure we read is its value, and the saving beside it recomputes as you type. Changing a figure turns the status into Save. The row still opens, for the supplier and Send to review.

Recommended
Why this one
  • The estimate is the thing you edit. One keystroke and the saving moves, which is the behaviour you asked for, with nothing to open first.
  • Save appears exactly where the change happened and the status answers it.
  • Fanny's page carries eight inputs, which reads as a form; her bands as placeholders keep it quiet until she types.
Version 3

One table

The journey in one frame. Today's rows first, a seam, then the rest with inputs in the spend cell. One set of columns for both: spend, saved or could save, status. The foot has two lines, today and elsewhere, so the two totals meet.

What to weigh
  • "Spend" and "Saved" change meaning across the seam: read and realised above it, estimated and projected below.
  • One frame, one scroll, and the two totals sit together at the foot.
  • The seam is the only thing keeping verified green away from estimated bands.
