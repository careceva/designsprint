1

Log in

Partial

User goalGet into the portal quickly — driven by a specific need, a notification, or wanting an overview.

Doing
  • Logs into the portal because they have a specific need, received a notification, or want an overview.
System / AI
  • Notifications should deep-link into the relevant view (savings, opportunity, agreement change), not just to the dashboard.
In the prototype
s-loginLogin — persona choice

Prototype login is a persona picker that lands straight on the right dashboard. Real authentication and notification deep-links are out of scope here.

Gaps
  • Notification → deep-link entry path is not demonstrated; every session starts at the dashboard.
BI Bob purposeful

Returning with intent — often triggered by a notification.

2

Connect spend data

Partial

User goalGive the product real data with the least effort and risk, so figures become exact instead of estimated.

Doing
  • Decides whether to connect to the ERP or upload a spend report.
System / AI
  • If connected to the ERP, the product can notice procurement made outside of agreements and suggest suppliers that fit.
  • Until data is connected, figures are peer-based estimates and clearly marked as such (estimated vs verified).
In the prototype
viewDataCenterData center — connect methods
viewNewCustomerRail action: 'Connect your accounting data'

v2-visual still ships the CSS for the dedicated onboarding screens (Sources, Loading/analyzing, Data set confirm, CEO welcome) but their markup was removed — the full connect flow lives in advantage-onboarding.html. Here, connecting is reachable via the Data center only.

Gaps
  • End-to-end connect flow (choose source → analyze → confirm data set → value reveal) is not walkable in this file.
  • Off-agreement procurement detection (the ERP payoff) is described but not shown.
BI Bob already done

Skips this stage — data already connected (all figures verified).

3

Understand current position

Built

User goalSee my procurement situation at a glance — agreements, suppliers, spend — without doing analysis myself.

Doing
  • Reviews the company's current agreements, suppliers, purchasing spend and overall procurement situation.
System / AI
  • Present what similar companies are doing based on AI analysis, so new customers never see a cold, empty start.
  • First login shows a one-time explainer for estimated vs verified data before a benchmark number can be mistaken for a real one.
In the prototype
viewDashSituation section + greeting
viewNewCustomerPeer benchmark dashboard (148 peers)
viewSupSuppliers
viewDashWelcome modal — estimated vs verified

The anti-cold-start decision from the sprint is implemented: Fanny lands on peer-based estimates for her industry and size.

Gaps

None — the Miro intent is covered.

BI Bob oriented

Verified agreements, suppliers and spend from his own invoices.

4

See realized & potential value

Built

User goalUnderstand what Advantage has already saved me and what's still on the table.

Doing
  • Uses the Savings Dashboard to understand realized savings, potential savings, spend through agreements and accumulated customer bonus.
  • Reviews notifications about new savings opportunities, agreement changes, achieved savings or decisions requiring attention.
System / AI
  • Realized vs potential is always shown as one story (the sketches' '7 mil NOK' reveal).
  • Notifications are relevance-filtered: new opportunities, agreement changes, achieved savings, decisions pending.
In the prototype
viewSavingsSavings dashboard
viewDashNotifications drawer
viewDashSavings toast

Realized, potential, spend-through-agreements and customer bonus are all present.

Gaps

None — the Miro intent is covered.

BI Bob value reveal — the peak

Peak moment of the journey — verified realized savings plus bonus.

5

Discover opportunity

Built

User goalFind where we can cut costs or get better terms — without hunting for it.

Doing
  • Sees proactively identified categories where the company could reduce costs, improve terms or move more spend to Advantage agreements.
  • Uses the AI Procurement Agent to understand opportunities, ask questions and identify where to focus next.
System / AI
  • Opportunities are proactively surfaced and ranked, not searched for.
  • The agent answers 'where should we focus next?' with reasons grounded in the company's own data.
In the prototype
viewDashOpportunities section + viz
viewDashWeekly discovery (nav hero: '4 new findings')
viewDashSaving assistant panel

Both discovery modes from the Miro board (proactive categories + conversational agent) are in the prototype.

Gaps

None — the Miro intent is covered.

BI Bob engaged

Weekly discovery cadence — findings since Monday.

6

Review business case

Built

User goalSee the concrete case for one opportunity: what we spend, what we'd save, and why this is relevant to us.

Doing
  • Reviews potential savings, current spend, recommended agreement, expected value and why the opportunity is relevant.
System / AI
  • Every recommendation explains 'why relevant' — grounded in the company's spend, not generic.
  • Method and timeline are available but folded away once read, keeping the saving and agreements in front.
In the prototype
viewOppOpportunity detail
viewOppBusiness case dialog (e.g. 1.2 mil NOK / yr)
viewCatTabCategory tab hero with saving

Matches the sketches' business-case card ('1.2 mil NOK / yr' → 'Ready to decide?').

Gaps

None — the Miro intent is covered.

BI Bob focused

Case built on verified spend.

7

Evaluate

Partial

User goalJudge whether switching supplier or agreement actually makes business sense for us.

Doing
  • Assesses whether changing supplier or agreement makes business sense based on savings, terms, risk, effort and other business considerations.
System / AI
  • The agent should answer evaluation questions (terms, risk, effort) — not only savings math.
In the prototype
viewOppMethod & timeline folds
viewDashAssistant follow-up questions

Savings and method are covered; the broader business considerations are only reachable by asking the agent.

Gaps
  • No explicit terms / risk / switching-effort comparison in the business case — the Miro board names these as decision inputs.
  • No side-by-side of current agreement vs recommended agreement.
BI Bob deliberate, slightly skeptical

May involve colleagues — the case needs to be shareable/exportable eventually.

8

Decide

Partial

User goalCommit with an appropriate level of effort: proceed, get an offer, approve the switch — or hand it to an advisor.

Doing
  • Decides whether to proceed with the opportunity, request an offer, approve a switch or involve an Advantage advisor.
System / AI
  • Every decision path is low-risk framed: requesting an offer or involving an advisor commits to nothing.
  • Decisions requiring attention resurface via notifications (stage 4) if parked.
In the prototype
viewOppDecision CTAs on opportunity
viewOppAdvisor contact block

CTAs and advisor path exist; what happens after the click is not shown.

Gaps
  • No post-decision state: confirmation, expected next steps, status tracking of a requested offer or switch.
  • No visible distinction in commitment level between 'request offer' and 'approve switch'.
BI Bob commitment tension

Wants self-serve approval with a clear undo window.

9

Prove value

Partial

User goalShow (to myself and internally) that the decisions paid off — and stay compliant while doing it.

Doing
  • Sees accumulated and paid customer bonus as an additional part of the value generated through the agreements.
  • Accesses relevant supplier and compliance documentation when needed, including documentation related to the Transparency Act.
System / AI
  • Bonus is presented as accumulated and paid — value beyond the price savings.
  • Compliance documentation (incl. Transparency Act / Åpenhetsloven) is retrievable per supplier when needed.
In the prototype
viewSavingsAccumulated & paid customer bonus

Bonus is in the savings dashboard; documentation is not in this prototype (Reports nav item is a stub).

Gaps
  • Supplier & compliance documentation (Transparency Act) has no screen anywhere in v2-visual.
  • No export/share of the value story for internal reporting.
BI Bob trust & pride

Uses bonus + realized savings as the renewal argument.

10

Prioritize next opportunity

Partial

User goalLet results and recommendations tell me what to optimize next — keep the loop going without effort.

Doing
  • Uses realized results and AI recommendations to decide which purchasing category or agreement to optimize next.
System / AI
  • The customer gets notified when new suppliers matching their spending data are added.
  • The customer is notified when savings potential is registered that isn't being utilized.
In the prototype
viewDashNext best actions rail
viewDashWeekly discovery findings
viewDashNotifications

The ingredients exist; the loop (this realized result → therefore this next recommendation) is not made explicit.

Gaps
  • New-supplier-match and unused-potential notifications are named on the board but not simulated.
  • Recommendations don't visibly reference realized results ('office supplies saved 19% — flooring shows the same pattern').
BI Bob confident, forward-looking

This stage closes his loop back to stage 4/5 on the next visit.

Use it in the prototype

Every stage carries the prototype view id and JS hook (e.g. viewSavings / openSavings()). Load journey.json in the prototype to drive a guided tour or scenario mode — step through stages, call the hook, highlight the view.

Use it as a backlog

Filter stages by coverage. Every partial card lists its gaps — those are the next prototype tasks (connect-data flow, evaluate comparison, post-decision state, compliance docs, closing the loop).

Keep it honest

Edit journey.json, not this file's markup — the map renders from the embedded copy of the same data. When the journey changes in Miro, change the JSON and re-embed (or ask Claude to).
