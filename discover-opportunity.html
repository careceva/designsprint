Sees a short, ranked set of opportunities built from their own spend and from what comparable companies do — and can question any of them in plain language before acting.

It keeps the original step's intent, and adds the two things the group actually put on the board that weren't in the first version: the comparison against other customers, and the fact that the recommendation is interrogable rather than take-it-or-leave-it.

Five things that weren't there this morning

01 · Worth resolving first

The flow you drew is an onboarding flow

Get more info → connect ERP → analyse → recommend is steps 2 and 3 of the journey, not step 5. In detailing “Discover opportunity” the group re-drew the data-acquisition path.

Two readings, and they lead to different products. Either the step boundary is wrong — or discovery genuinely can't happen without a data top-up each time, in which case every cycle of the loop asks the customer for something, and that's a very different experience from “it just tells you”.

02 · Strongest idea on the board

The adjacency rule

“If the customer uses travel agreements but no hotel, suggest starting with hotel.”

Concrete, explainable, and it needs no new data — you already know which agreements are active. It's also the easiest thing here to put in front of someone on Friday and get a clean yes or no. If one idea from this board gets prototyped, make it this one.

03 · Changes what this is

A sales rep appeared in the background lane

“Notifies the sales rep if a manual check is needed.” That's the first internal human in the machine, and it quietly makes this a hybrid service rather than an automated one.

It also lines up with what both markets asked for independently — a person, not only a system. Worth making deliberate rather than incidental: what triggers the handoff, what the rep sees, and whether the customer is told it happened.

04 · Needs a decision

There are now two agents

“Opens chat with AI” and “starts the onboarding agent” are separate stickies. So is this one assistant with two modes, or two agents with different jobs and different tones?

Cheap to decide now, expensive later — it shapes the whole interface. My instinct: one assistant, two moments, because a customer who meets two different AIs in their first week will trust neither.

05 · A tension to name

The AI asks the customer questions

“Has a conversation about their habits and asks relevant follow-up questions”, plus “get more info from customer”. So the system interrogates the user to sharpen its recommendations.

That sits directly against the other group's finding — avoid cold starts, don't make people fill things in. Both can be true, but only if every question is earned: ask after showing value, never before, and never ask twice for something you could infer.

What I'd carry into Wednesday

QuestionWhy it matters, and how to settle it
Does discovery need fresh data every time? If yes, the loop has an ask built into every cycle and the product is much heavier than it looks. If no, move the ERP/spend-report flow back to step 2 and let step 5 stay pure discovery. Settle this before sketching — it changes the storyboard's first frame.
One agent or two? Onboarding agent and procurement chat as separate things doubles the surface and halves the trust. Pick one and give it two moments.
When does the sales rep get pulled in? Define the trigger, what they see, and whether the customer knows. An invisible human handoff is a feature; a silent one is a surprise.
How many questions may the AI ask? Suggest a hard rule for the prototype — no questions before the first piece of value is on screen — and test whether people answer them at all.
Is the peer comparison a headline or a footnote? It shows up in both the flow and the background lane, so the group clearly rates it. Decide whether it drives the recommendation or merely supports it — that's a layout decision on Wednesday.
