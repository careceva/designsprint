Two portals, one teardown

You suspected the v177 prototype and advantage-main.html had drifted into near-duplicates. They haven't. They are two different products, built for two different markets, that disagree about where the hard part of procurement actually is.

The thing to settle first

These aren't two versions. They're two markets.

Before comparing a single flow: the files don't share a locale, a currency format, a supplier list, or an advisor. Any "which flow is better" conversation has to decide whether one of these is being retired — or whether both ship and the flows only need to rhyme.

Flow 01

Entry & identity

Who am I, and what state is my account in?

  • Login card demo@advantage.no, any credentials
  • Choose company — three profiles with their test purpose spelled out
  • Nordic Build (8 agreements) · Fjord Labs (0, new) · Orbit Works (10, no bonus)
  • Portal boots — help rail opens itself on desktop
  • Switch company any time from the top bar

The no-bonus profile is the sharp idea here: it forces the value story to hold up when cashback is zero, and it hides the Cashback nav item entirely rather than showing an empty page.

3 profilesconditional navtop-bar switcher

  • Welcome overlay — explains estimated vs. verified before you can misread a number
  • New customer sees ranges, not figures — enforced in code by saveFig
  • State persists across reloads via persist()
  • switchPersona() and startOver() for demoing

Only two personas, but the trust problem is solved structurally rather than with copy: a benchmark literally cannot render as a hard number for Fanny.

v177 · but steal one thing

Three companies beats two personas, and the no-bonus profile is a genuinely good test case. But main's welcome overlay is the better answer to the same anxiety v177 handles with scattered "estimated / documented" labels — and main enforces it in code where v177 relies on the writer remembering.

Flow 02

The landing screen

What do I do this morning?

  • Hero — 210 000 kr total value, savings/bonus ring
  • Where to unlock more value — three ranked cards
  • What changed — activity feed, three rows, each with a result column
  • Current offers → Popular agreements → New & updated
  • We're here when you need us — Maja and Advantage AI

Reads top-to-bottom like a newsletter. Six sections, all of them push. Strong on social proof — "popular", "most used", "new supplier" — which main has almost none of on the surface.

social proof railoffers modulestatic feed

  • Situation grid — saved YTD, scaled by how much of the year has elapsed
  • Opportunity by agreement — every un-activated one, biggest saving first
  • Activity rail (right) — next best actions on top, then the log of what we did for you
  • Rail collapses to a mini state and remembers it

Reads like an operations console. The rail is the real difference: it's persistent across pages, and it separates "what you should do" from "what already happened" — v177 merges both into one feed on one page.

main

v177's home is better marketing; main's is better software. A returning member's question is "what needs me today", and only main answers it in a place that follows you around. v177's social proof and Offers modules are worth keeping — they just belong beside a rail, not instead of one.

Flow 03

Finding an opportunity

I need something. Where do I start?

  • Needs intake — free textarea: "We send ~200 packages a month and want to cut shipping costs"
  • Example chips prefill it; interpretNeed() keyword-matches to an area
  • Result: "This looks like Shipping" + supplier count + two exits
  • No match → route the need straight to the advisor
  • — OR — six personalised opportunity cards, ranked, with kr ranges
  • All areas — 25-item searchable directory

Two doors, explicitly labelled with an OR divider: describe your need, or browse what we picked for you. Global ⌘K search is a third door from anywhere.

free-text intakeOR divider⌘K global search25-area directory
  • Weekly discovery — hero nav card, "4 new findings since Monday"
  • Areas — recommended picks first, then sort by potential or name
  • Opportunity by agreement on the dashboard, money-ranked
  • Ask the assistant — plain-language question in the advisor pane
  • Opening one shows how the number was worked out before you commit

Everything is push or ranked-by-money. The assistant is the only free-text door, and it's a chat panel rather than a structured intake that produces a routed result.

Weekly discovery pushassistant chatno needs intakeno global search
v177, clearly

main assumes the member arrives without an agenda and waits to be told what's valuable. Half of them arrive with a job already in hand. v177's intake plus ⌘K search covers both; main covers one. This is the single biggest structural gap in main.

Flow 04

The category page

I'm in an area. Help me narrow it down.

  • Breadcrumb → headline framed as a commercial claim, not a category name
  • Potential saving with an ⓘ why popover explaining the benchmark
  • Three numbered benefits — why this area is worth your attention
  • Progressive needs discovery — one question at a time, four total, then a summary chip row
  • → ranked recommendations with "best match" and a reason per supplier
  • → Send my request, or ask Maja first
  • Active variant: realised savings + "Are you happy with Selecta?" → reason chips

The page is a funnel: qualify, then recommend, then request. The satisfaction check on already-used agreements is a nice touch — it treats "keep what works" as a valid outcome.

  • Six alternative layout styles behind the scenes — bars, brands, focus, split, story, why
  • Contact the group's category buyer from inside the tab

Splits two things v177 merges: understanding a deal, and acting on it. Opening in a tab means you can keep a category open while working elsewhere. But nothing qualifies your need first — you get the same page whoever you are.

v177 knows who's asking; main knows what the page is for. The best version is v177's four-question qualifier feeding main's Deal/Activate tab structure — qualify once, then land on a page that separates reading from doing. Neither ships that today.

The strongest work in either prototype. "How do you book today?" branches the rollout advice three ways. And the code lives on the page with instructions — the one thing that decides whether an agreement gets used.

Flow 06

Activation — the commit

I said yes. Now what happens?

  • Request agreement code — one supplier at a time
  • State flips to requested; card becomes "being prepared"
  • Toast confirms; an Inbox thread appears
  • Inbox shows request sent to N suppliers with per-supplier response status
  • When ready: code appears in Resources → Access wallet

The request is tracked and the destination is clear. What's missing is the middle: no dates, no expectations, and nothing ever tells you the saving actually landed.

per-supplier response statusno datesno verification stepone at a time
  • Select several agreements, activate as a batch
  • Funnel screen: "Sending 3 requests…" with a spinner, then done
  • Four-step stepper, each step carrying a real date:
  • 1 · Request sent — today. "Nothing changes on your side yet."
  • 2 · Supplier confirms — within 5 working days
  • 3 · New price is live — ~2 weeks
  • 4 · Saving verified — "we read the next invoice and check the rate matches"

This is the best flow in either file. Step 4 is the whole product: it closes the loop between a promise and an invoice, and it's the only place either prototype admits that activation isn't the same as saving.

batch activationdated 4-step stepperinvoice verification
main, decisively

v177 treats activation as a form submission. main treats it as a two-week process with a verifiable end. Everything in v177's Value page is a claim until something like step 4 exists — and v177 has no equivalent anywhere.

Flow 07

Asking for better terms

The price still isn't right. What are my options?

  • Message the advisor with a prefilled draft
  • Or request quotes from several suppliers on the same brief
  • Upload an invoice so the comparison is grounded
  • That's the full set of levers

Every route is "switch, or get another quote". There is no way to push back on a supplier you already use and intend to keep.

multi-supplier quote requestno price challengeno terms negotiation
  • Price challenge — openPriceChallenge(r, a)
  • Pick a reason (competitor), name the rival and their price
  • AI assembles three assertions from data you already hold: annual spend, invoice count, member count, effective rate
  • → drafted challenge letter → review → send
  • Custom request — choose terms (delivery, payment…), pick recipients, add a note, get a draft

Two distinct negotiation instruments. The price challenge is notable for what it doesn't do: it invents nothing. Every claim in the draft traces to something the app can actually prove.

price challengeevidence from held datacustom terms request
main — no contest

This is a whole product capability v177 doesn't have. A buying group's leverage is collective volume, and main is the only one that turns that volume into a sentence you can send a supplier. Port both flows.

Flow 08

Proving the value

What has this membership actually returned?

  • Value page — period selector: 12 months, YTD, last year, custom
  • Three KPIs with trend pills vs. the same period last year
  • Ring (savings vs. bonus) + category contribution bars
  • Category accordion → open one → supplier rows with savings / bonus / spend
  • Cashback — its own page: total, split by year, accruing vs. in payout, per-supplier share bars
  • Nav item disappears entirely for the no-bonus company

Cashback as a first-class destination is right — members feel a payout differently from a discount. The period selector and trend pills are also better than main's equivalent.

More analytically serious — exact splits, month-by-month shares, a usage report that asks whether agreements are being used rather than just whether they exist. But bonus is folded into "value", not surfaced as its own thing.

Flow 09

Renewal & drift

What's about to expire, and what quietly stopped working?

  • "Agreements to review" strip on the Agreements page
  • Rows show "Agreement period — not registered"
  • → Add period modal: start date, end/renewal date, notice period
  • Then Advantage can remind you

Honest about the gap, but the work is the member's. Nothing detects drift on its own — an agreement that's live and unused looks identical to one that's live and working.

notice-period fieldmanual entryno drift detection
  • Renewal calendar — month and year views, per-agreement renewal dates
  • Dormant detection — Hilti Erhverv and Lyreco Erhverv flagged "Live but unused"
  • agQuietDays — days since the last invoice came through that agreement
  • Nav badge counts activated agreements that still need something
  • Three portfolio views: basket · compare · impact

"Live but unused" is the sharpest diagnosis in either prototype — a signed agreement that nobody orders through is the most common and most invisible failure in a buying group, and only main names it.

renewal calendar"Live but unused"quiet-days counterlive nav badge
main

v177 asks the member to do the noticing. main does the noticing and then tells them. Combine: keep v177's notice-period field (main has renewal dates but no notice window, which is the number that actually matters for switching), and adopt main's dormancy flag wholesale.

Flow 10

Getting people to use it

The agreement is live. How does it reach 40 employees?

  • Access wallet — the code, big, with a copy button
  • "How to use" collapsible: copy the code → add to each traveller profile → book
  • Company discount page URL employees can bookmark
  • Adoption cards: create employee instructions · draft a travel policy with AI · brief the travel agency
  • Team & access — roles, permissions, pending invites

Treats adoption as a distinct job with its own surfaces. The bookmarkable employee page is the pragmatic bit — most staff will never log into a procurement portal.

access walletemployee URLpolicy draftingroles & invites
  • Usage appears as a Reports type — you can see adoption is low
  • Dormancy flag tells you an agreement isn't being ordered through
  • …and then the product stops

main is excellent at detecting the adoption problem and has no tooling to fix it. There is no code surface, no employee-facing artifact, and no user management.

usage detectionno code surfaceno employee artifactno team management
v177

The two sides are the halves of one loop: main measures adoption, v177 drives it. Shipping either alone means you either can't see the problem or can't act on it. This pairing is the most obviously additive in the whole teardown.

Better at presence — one rail, everywhere, context-aware, and it doesn't obscure the thing you're asking about. Weaker at specificity: Maja is a stock advisor with no visible knowledge of your account.

The read

They're not competing. They're two halves of one product.

v177 owns the front half — discover, qualify, choose, get access, get your colleagues using it. main owns the back half — commit, negotiate, verify, renew, catch drift. Almost nothing overlaps in a way that forces a choice, which is why they felt similar: they're both good, in different places.

What we need to decide before merging anything

  • Are both markets shipping, or is one lineage being retired? This is upstream of every other call. A shared codebase with locale switching is a very different build from two portals that merely share patterns — and right now they share no data model, currency format, supplier list, or advisor.
  • Is our core promise "find you a better deal" or "make sure the deal you signed is working"? v177 is built for the first, main for the second. The merged IA has to lead with one. My read: main's promise is the defensible one — anyone can publish a discount list, but verifying it landed is the thing a member can't do alone.
  • Ten destinations or six? v177's page-per-job is more discoverable but thinner per page; main's six-item rail with overlays is denser but hides a lot. Merging both feature sets into v177's IA gives ~14 destinations, which is too many.
  • Does activation stay one-at-a-time, or become a batch? main's batch funnel is faster, but v177's per-supplier response tracking is what a member actually watches afterwards. These need reconciling, not just picking.
