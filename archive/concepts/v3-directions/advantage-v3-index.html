Five answers to “dashboard × catalogue”

Same brief, same data, same design system — five different opinions about what the product is. All five honor the agreed IA: Categories and Agreements as separate sections, Savings and Spending as separate pages. Each challenges the brief from a different angle. Every version is fully navigable with the Bob ↔ Fanny persona switch (verified vs estimated data) in the account menu.

1advantage-v3-1-marketplace.html

The Marketplace

A dashboard reports; a catalogue sells. Savings are merchandised like products — shelves, price tags, an “add to plan” tray. The overview is a storefront of negotiated prices you're not using yet.

ChallengesIf browsing is the core loop, the KPI dashboard can shrink to a strip. Do we dare make it feel like shopping?

The Ledger

Savings only mean something against spend. The overview is one money statement — spend flows in, savings and bonus flow out. Savings and Spending stay separate pages but deliberately mirror each other, line by line.

ChallengesSplitting savings and spending into separate pages risks hiding their causal link — this version keeps the split but refuses to break the connection.

Open prototype → 3advantage-v3-3-category-spine.html

The Category Spine

Categories are the product's spine. One expandable category board is both dashboard and catalogue. Agreements keep their own section — but as a thin registry that always deep-links back into category context.

ChallengesCategories and Agreements as equal siblings invites building two catalogues. An agreement outside its category is just a PDF.

Open prototype → 4advantage-v3-4-decision-queue.html

The Decision Queue

Browsing is optional; deciding is the job. The overview is an inbox of prepared decisions with three commitment levels (approve · request offer · ask advisor). The catalogue is reference material behind the queue.

ChallengesAn SMB owner gives this 10 minutes a week. Is a catalogue to explore actually what they need — or six decisions ready to go?

Open prototype → 5advantage-v3-5-cfo-spend.html

The CFO View

SMBs think in spend, not savings. Spending is the richest page; the overview reads like a monthly management report with a generated headline. Savings is the audit note that proves the story — screenshot-ready for the board meeting.

ChallengesLeading with the savings number is our framing, not the customer's. Spend is the language they already speak.

How to compare: run the same errand in each — “office supplies looks expensive, what do I do about it?” — and notice which version gets you from question to action fastest, then switch to Fanny and see which one would convince a new customer to connect data. The journey spec in advantage-ux/journey.json is the yardstick: stages 5–8 (discover → decide) are where these five differ most.
